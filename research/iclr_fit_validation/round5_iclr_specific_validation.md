# Round 5 — ICLR-Specific Fit Validation

> Status: **RESEARCH DECISION DOCUMENT**  
> Scope: ICLR only.  
> Date: 2026-09-06  
> Question: Is the current research direction capable of becoming a strong ICLR machine-learning paper, and what exact claim should we test?

---

## 0. Executive decision

### Original broad claim: HOLD / DO NOT TEST AS THE MAIN PAPER

> Shared/relevance-ranked retrieval causes scientific-idea monoculture.

This formulation is too broad and now has two problems:

1. **ICLR 2025 counterevidence:** Si et al., *Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?*, explicitly varied the number of RAG papers `k ∈ {0,5,10,20}` and reported almost unchanged near-duplicate diversity: `18.8, 18.4, 19.1, 19.4%` non-duplicates. They further report that adding RAG had minimal impact under that metric.
2. **2026 direct-neighbor work:** a July 2026 preprint, *Prompt language as a diversity lever*, shows that changing query language changes retrieved sources and downstream scientific proposals in one mechanobiology RAG system. Therefore the broad statement “retrieval diversity propagates to idea diversity” is no longer a sufficiently distinctive claim.

### Revised ICLR hypothesis: CONDITIONAL GO

The strongest ICLR-shaped question now is:

> **At fixed retrieval quantity, topical relevance, prompt, model, and token budget, does the composition of retrieved evidence act as an inference-time prior that changes the structural distribution of scientific hypotheses generated by an LLM?**

A sharper version is:

> **Can evidence composition strongly steer *which scientific questions are explored* while leaving coarse text-level duplicate rates nearly unchanged?**

This turns the accepted ICLR 2025 RAG-null result into a useful baseline rather than an obstacle.

### Proposed working title

**Same Relevance, Different Science: Evidence Composition as an Inference-Time Prior over LLM Hypothesis Search**

Alternatives:

- **Beyond RAG Quantity: How Evidence Composition Shapes LLM Hypothesis Search**
- **Evidence as a Prior: Causal Effects of Retrieval Composition on Scientific Hypothesis Generation**
- stretch version only if the MUSES experiment succeeds: **Relevance Is Not Discovery Utility: What Literature Should Research Agents Read?**

---

## 1. Why this can fit ICLR

ICLR 2027's reviewer guide asks whether a submission tackles a specific question, is well motivated in the literature, supports its claims rigorously, and contributes meaningful new knowledge. It explicitly states that SOTA is not required and asks reviewers to be open to interesting/surprising results and new directions.

This project can satisfy that standard if it is presented as **LLM inference behavior**, not as a sociological claim about researchers:

```text
retrieved evidence composition
        ↓  controlled intervention
LLM in-context state / research framing
        ↓
hypothesis distribution
        ↓
problem × mechanism × method × evaluation choices
```

The ML object is the conditional distribution produced by the model under a controlled context intervention. The scientific-community interpretation is optional external motivation, not the primary empirical object.

### ICLR precedent

**Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity? — ICLR 2024**

This paper demonstrates that ICLR can value a population-level diversity phenomenon when the experiment is controlled and the mechanism is isolated. It compares human writing with GPT-3/InstructGPT assistance and traces the homogenization primarily to the model-contributed text.

**Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? — ICLR 2025**

This paper makes scientific ideation itself a legitimate ICLR evaluation target, uses >100 expert researchers, and identifies diversity and self-evaluation as open problems.

**STARS — ICLR 2026**

This paper directly treats homogeneous LM generation as mode collapse and proposes an inference-time mechanism for diverse trajectories, including scientific-discovery evaluation. Thus generic “increase generation diversity” is already crowded.

**Mixing Mechanisms — ICLR 2026**

This is a useful paper-shape archetype: identify that a simple prior mechanism does not explain behavior, isolate multiple mechanisms with controlled experiments, then construct a causal model that predicts behavior. Our work should aspire to this form rather than another research-agent framework.

**REGENT — ICLR 2025**

REGENT explicitly calls retrieval a powerful bias for adaptation. Therefore the generic statement “retrieval is an inductive bias” is not novel. Our claim must be specific to **evidence composition inducing a structured prior over open-ended hypothesis search**.

## 2. The most important new evidence: ICLR 2025 already tested RAG quantity

Si et al. Appendix A.29 reports:

| RAG papers added (`k`) | Non-Duplicates (%) |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 18.8 |
| 5 | 18.4 |
| 10 | 19.1 |
| 20 | 19.4 |

They state that varying `k` has **minimal impact on diversity**. Their broader prompt ablation similarly reports:

| Setup | Non-Duplicates (%) |
|---|---:|
| No RAG; No previous ideas | 7.6 |
| No RAG; previous ideas included | 18.8 |
| RAG k=5; previous ideas | 18.4 |
| RAG k=10; previous ideas | 19.1 |
| RAG k=20; previous ideas | 19.4 |

They also find very large backbone effects:

| Backbone | Non-Duplicates (%) |
|---|---:|
| Claude-3.5-Sonnet | 19.1 |
| GPT-4o | 59.5 |
| o1-mini | 22.6 |
| Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct | 51.1 |

Their generation system randomly samples 10 papers from the top 20 retrieved papers when RAG is used. Their diversity analysis treats an idea as duplicate if its all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embedding cosine similarity is >0.8 to a previous idea.

### Why this is not fatal

That experiment varies primarily **amount/presence of RAG**, while our proposed experiment varies **which matched evidence is supplied**.

The near-duplicate metric answers roughly:

> “Does RAG make generated proposal text cross a semantic duplicate threshold?”

Our proposed structural question is different:

> “Holding relevance and amount fixed, does evidence composition redistribute probability mass among different scientific problems, assumptions, mechanisms, methods, and evaluation targets?”

Both can simultaneously be true:

```text
near-duplicate rate:       ≈ unchanged
hypothesis-mode allocation: strongly changed
```

That dissociation would itself be a strong ICLR result because it reveals a blind spot in the current way ideation diversity is measured.

## 3. Collision map

### 3.1 Direct / dangerous neighbors

#### Si et al., ICLR 2025 — Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?

**Overlap:** scientific ideation, RAG, diversity, large-scale generation.  
**Our required distinction:** composition rather than quantity/presence; structural distribution rather than duplicate threshold; causal matched-evidence intervention.

#### Prompt language as a diversity lever (preprint, July 2026)

A single-domain study uses multilingual queries over a 523-document mechanobiology corpus. English queries repeatedly retrieved two papers; translated prompts surfaced 25 papers across languages, and downstream proposals changed in mechanism and experimental design.

**Overlap:** retrieval-source diversity propagating downstream to scientific proposals.  
**Important weaknesses we must not inherit:** one scientific question/domain, prompt language simultaneously changes retrieval geometry and possibly generation behavior, no matched-relevance evidence intervention, and primarily lexical/content diversity analyses.

**Our distinction:** directly intervene on the retrieved packet itself while keeping the generator prompt language identical; matched relevance/year/quality; multi-domain and multi-model; test dose-response; structural outcome labels.

#### RQ-Bench / novelty-mirage preprint (June 2026)

Finds model-generated research questions are often narrow or source-bound, and LLM novelty judges fail to recognize this reliably.

**Overlap:** source-bound scientific questions.  
**Our distinction:** RQ-Bench diagnoses the output and judge; we test a causal upstream mechanism — whether evidence composition *produces* source-boundedness and which evidence dimensions transfer into hypotheses.

### 3.2 Crowded but non-direct neighbors

#### STARS, ICLR 2026

Generation-side activation steering for diverse trajectories, including scientific discovery.

**Implication:** do not make “a better diversity method” the main paper. Instead use generation-diversity methods as controls: can they broaden within-context variation while leaving evidence-induced directional bias intact?

#### IDEAgent / Heuresis / other quality-diversity scientific ideation systems (2026)

These explicitly search for diverse/high-quality ideas.

**Implication:** avoid another agentic QD framework. Mechanism discovery is the differentiator.

#### SciPIP / Graph2Idea / SCI-IDEA / IdeaBench

These improve or benchmark literature-grounded scientific ideation using richer retrieval, graphs, structured facets, or contextual evaluation.

**Implication:** “better literature context improves ideation” is not novel. We need controlled causal evidence about **how context composition redistributes the hypothesis search space**.

### 3.3 High-value neighboring evidence

#### MUSES (Aug 31, 2026)

MUSES evaluates prospective retrieval of intellectual roots rather than ordinary relevance/citations. It contains author-endorsed generative-inspiration labels. Hit@100 drops strongly from ordinary/familiar citation targets to author-endorsed roots.

This creates a high-upside extension:

> If ordinary relevance is not the same as finding generative roots, are generative roots also more causally useful to downstream hypothesis generation than relevance-matched non-roots?

MUSES itself studies retrieval. It does **not** establish downstream causal discovery utility for an LLM ideation system.

## 4. Revised formal problem

Let:

- `x`: research topic / seed question
- `M`: frozen LLM generator
- `C`: packet of retrieved evidence
- `H`: structured scientific hypothesis representation

The model induces:

`P_M(H | x, C)`.

We are not primarily interested in whether `C` changes surface text. We want to estimate whether controlled changes to the **composition** of `C` alter the distribution over interpretable hypothesis modes.

Define a structured hypothesis tuple:

```text
H = (
  problem_object,
  assumed_or_observed_failure,
  mechanism_or_explanation,
  intervention_or_method,
  evaluation_target
)
```

Primary causal estimand for matched packets `C_a` and `C_b`:

```text
Δ_structure = D( P(H | x, C_a), P(H | x, C_b) )
```

where `D` is pre-specified using multiple non-equivalent measures (e.g. Jensen–Shannon divergence over human-audited categories, collision/entropy metrics, and semantic distribution distance).

### Stronger directional hypothesis

If an evidence packet overrepresents a framing dimension `z` (e.g. failure diagnosis, memory, optimization, evaluation, mechanistic explanation), the probability of generated hypotheses using the same or adjacent framing should increase:

```text
P(H contains z | C enriched in z)
  >
P(H contains z | matched C depleted in z)
```

This is much more interpretable than a global “diversity score.”

## 5. Paper shapes considered

### Shape A — Diversity measurement paper

**Claim:** RAG research agents become homogeneous.  
**Decision:** Reject as main direction. Too close to existing diversity literature and too easy to call obvious.

### Shape B — Evidence-composition mechanism paper — PRIMARY

**Claim:** Evidence composition is a structured inference-time prior over open-ended hypothesis generation; its influence can be large even when coarse duplicate diversity is unchanged.

**ICLR fit:** High if the controlled effect is robust and interpretable.  
**Needed evidence:** matched-relevance intervention, cross-model/domain replication, structural labels, confound controls, mechanism/predictive analysis.

### Shape C — Relevance vs discovery utility — STRETCH

**Claim:** Relevance-oriented retrieval optimizes the wrong target for discovery; author-endorsed generative roots have distinct downstream causal utility.

**ICLR fit:** Potentially very high.  
**Risk:** temporal contamination, historical reconstruction validity, defining “future direction” without circular scoring.

### Shape D — New diversity-aware retrieval algorithm

**Decision:** Secondary only. A simple mitigation is useful, but another scientific-agent/RAG framework is too crowded to be the main novelty.

## 6. Experiment program

### E0 — Reproduce the ICLR 2025 RAG-quantity null baseline

Purpose: establish compatibility with the nearest accepted ICLR baseline.

Conditions:

```text
k = 0, 5, 10, 20
```

Use the same or closely matched near-duplicate metric as Si et al. as one reported outcome.

**Expected / acceptable result:** near-duplicate diversity changes little with `k`.

This is not a failed pilot. It establishes the phenomenon our structural metric is intended to go beyond.

---

### E1 — Matched-Relevance Composition Intervention — CORE

For a fixed topic and model:

- fixed `k` (e.g. 12 papers)
- fixed relevance-score histogram/bin counts
- fixed year distribution
- fixed venue tier if used
- matched length/token budget
- match citation/popularity where metadata permits
- identical generator prompt
- identical prompt language
- randomized document order

Construct multiple evidence packets with different compositions.

#### E1a — Controlled source-overlap dose response

Across independent agent runs, enforce source overlap approximately:

```text
0% / 25% / 50% / 75% / 100%
```

The unique replacement papers must come from matched relevance strata.

Primary question:

> Does hypothesis-space convergence increase monotonically or predictably with shared evidence exposure?

#### E1b — Framing-balanced packet swap

Build matched packets enriched for different literature framings while holding topic relevance similar, for example:

```text
method improvement
failure / stress testing
evaluation / measurement
mechanistic explanation
efficiency / optimization
```

Primary question:

> Do generated hypotheses inherit the framing composition of the supplied evidence?

This is more interpretable than simple source overlap and gives a mechanism-level result.

---

### E2 — Metric-dissociation experiment

Directly test whether coarse duplicate metrics miss context-induced steering.

For every controlled condition report simultaneously:

1. Si-style near-duplicate rate;
2. semantic pairwise distribution;
3. structured tuple distribution;
4. human-audited categorical divergence.

The strongest outcome would be:

```text
near-duplicate rate ≈ invariant
BUT
problem/method/framing allocation shifts strongly by context
```

This would explain why prior RAG-quantity ablations can appear null while evidence still strongly steers scientific search.

---

### E3 — Mechanism / mediation

Do not claim internal mechanistic interpretability unless we actually inspect activations. For a black-box paper, use an operational causal mechanism:

```text
evidence features
   ↓
context framing exposure
   ↓
generated RQ / method framing
```

Analysis:

- annotate/extract evidence packet features;
- estimate transfer coefficients from evidence feature proportions to generated-hypothesis feature probabilities;
- swap one matched evidence cluster at a time;
- test held-out topic prediction: can packet composition predict which hypothesis mode the model chooses?

A predictive model that generalizes to held-out topics would materially strengthen the “structured prior” claim.

---

### E4 — Generation-side diversity controls

The critical alternative explanation is that the effect is just generic model mode collapse.

Controls:

- low vs high temperature;
- multi-model generation;
- if reproducible/available, a generation-diversity method such as STARS or a simpler established alternative;
- prompt paraphrases and document-order randomization.

Strong result:

> Generation-side methods broaden within-packet variation, but different matched evidence packets still induce systematically shifted hypothesis distributions.

This separates **how broadly the model samples** from **where the model samples**.

---

### E5 — MUSES Generative-Root Injection — HIGH-RISK/HIGH-UPSIDE

For a historical focal paper and an author-endorsed intellectual root `r+`, construct a relevance-matched non-root `r-`.

Conditions:

```text
C0 = historical background only
C1 = historical background + r-
C2 = historical background + r+
```

Generate candidate RQs/method sketches without exposing the focal paper.

Evaluate against pre-registered structured descriptors of the eventual focal contribution, not only with an LLM judge.

Main estimand:

```text
DiscoveryUtility(root) - DiscoveryUtility(matched relevant non-root)
```

#### Critical leakage issue

Do not run this experiment naively with frontier models that may have memorized the focal paper. Options must be studied before execution:

- focal papers after a model's verified training cutoff;
- older frozen open models whose data cutoff predates focal work;
- strong masking of names/datasets plus contamination diagnostics;
- author-disjoint / time-safe MUSES splits where applicable.

If leakage cannot be credibly controlled, E5 remains motivation/future work rather than paper evidence.

## 7. Outcome measurement

### 7.1 Do not use an LLM novelty judge as ground truth

Recent RQ-Bench results report a “novelty mirage”: LLM judges prefer model-generated RQs while domain experts often prefer author-anchored questions; generated RQs are frequently narrow/source-bound.

Therefore:

- LLM parsing is allowed for scalable structured extraction;
- all primary category definitions must be fixed before looking at outcome differences;
- audit a stratified sample manually / with qualified humans;
- report parser agreement/error;
- do not make “novelty score from GPT-X” a primary result.

### 7.2 Primary metric family

Use at least three independent views:

**A. Categorical structural distribution**

- problem object
- failure/assumption
- method family
- evaluation target
- research mode: extension / combination / diagnosis / falsification / measurement / mechanism / replication / null hypothesis

Metrics:

- entropy / effective number of categories
- Jensen–Shannon divergence between conditions
- collision rate
- mutual information `I(context_condition; hypothesis_mode)`

**B. Semantic distribution**

- pairwise distances
- cluster entropy
- distributional distance across condition centroids / optimal transport where justified

**C. Human audit**

Blind raters judge whether pairs are substantively pursuing the same scientific question/method, not whether wording is similar.

### 7.3 Quality controls

Diversity without quality is not a win. Record at minimum:

- feasibility
- relevance to seed problem
- specificity/testability
- technical depth

Prefer human or cross-family assessment on a subset rather than a same-model self-judge.

---

## 8. Statistical design principles

1. Unit of inference is an independently sampled generation, grouped by topic/model/context packet.
2. Use hierarchical/bootstrap uncertainty over topics and seeds, not only pooled pairwise samples.
3. Report effect sizes, not only p-values.
4. Predefine category mappings and primary contrasts.
5. Prompt paraphrase/document order are nuisance factors; estimate their variance as a noise floor.
6. The composition effect must exceed the prompt/order noise floor to support the main claim.
7. Avoid pseudo-replication from thousands of pairwise similarities computed over a small number of independent generations.
8. Preserve all raw generations and parse failures.

## 9. Reviewer red-team

### R1 — “This is obvious: same papers produce same ideas.”

A publishable answer requires the experiment to show more than identical-input determinism:

- packets have matched relevance and equal size;
- context sets are semantically valid alternatives;
- controlled overlap yields a dose response;
- structural mode allocation shifts beyond prompt/order noise;
- preferably a model predicts the shift from evidence composition.

### R2 — “This is just prompt sensitivity.”

Controls:

- generator prompt is identical;
- same prompt language;
- evidence order randomized;
- paraphrase noise estimated separately;
- direct context-packet intervention rather than query-language changes.

### R3 — “Si et al. already found RAG has minimal effect.”

This should be confronted in the introduction, not hidden.

Our hypothesis predicts that their coarse duplicate metric can remain almost unchanged because it measures duplicate *surface proposals*, not redistribution among structured hypothesis modes. E0 deliberately attempts to reproduce their null; E1/E2 test the missing composition/structure axis.

### R4 — “Prompt-language diversity work already shows source diversity changes proposals.”

That paper manipulates query language, which simultaneously changes retrieval embeddings and may change generation behavior. It uses one domain/question and does not match relevance. We directly randomize/match retrieved evidence while leaving generation language fixed, then estimate dose response across models and domains.

### R5 — “Source-boundness is already known from RQ-Bench.”

RQ-Bench establishes a symptom and evaluator failure. We ask whether source-boundedness is causally driven by context composition and quantify feature transfer from evidence to hypothesis.

### R6 — “Your diversity metrics are arbitrary.”

The paper must stand on structural categories + blind human audit + multiple metric families. Embedding similarity alone cannot support the claim.

### R7 — “This is science-of-science, not machine learning.”

Do not claim effects on human science unless we run a human study. The primary object is `P_M(H | x,C)` and the intervention is an inference-time input to an ML model. The Nature-level monoculture evidence belongs only in motivation/discussion.

### R8 — “Different model backbones dominate the effect.”

This is plausible: Si et al. already show large backbone differences. We must report model×context interactions and demonstrate that evidence composition produces a reproducible within-model effect rather than pretending backbone does not matter.

## 10. Hard scientific gates

### KILL / major pivot if any of the following holds

1. Matched evidence composition does not shift structural hypothesis distributions beyond prompt/order noise across >=2 strong model families.
2. Observed differences disappear after matching relevance/topic/year/length and are explained entirely by lower evidence quality.
3. Effects are lexical only; blind raters see no substantive problem/method shift.
4. Structural labels are not reproducible enough to support distributional claims.
5. A new/direct prior paper is found that already performs matched evidence-packet interventions and measures hypothesis-level structural transfer at comparable scale.

### CONTINUE if most of the following hold

1. >=2 model families show a reproducible composition effect.
2. >=3 ICLR-relevant subareas show the effect.
3. Context effect exceeds prompt/order perturbation noise.
4. E0 reproduces or is compatible with the prior RAG-quantity near-null while E1/E2 reveal a structural effect.
5. Evidence-feature enrichment predicts hypothesis-feature enrichment.
6. Human audit agrees that the shift is substantive.
7. Quality is not catastrophically reduced by contexts that broaden exploration.

### PROMOTE TO HIGH-AMBITION PAPER if

The MUSES root-vs-matched-relevance experiment survives leakage controls and shows author-endorsed generative roots have reliably different downstream discovery utility.

## 11. What the successful paper should look like

### Figure 1 — The surprising dissociation

```text
RAG amount / duplicate rate       ≈ flat
Evidence composition / RQ mode    = large shift
```

Message: existing diversity measurement misses a consequential form of search-space steering.

### Figure 2 — Controlled exposure dose response

```text
shared/matched evidence overlap → hypothesis-mode convergence
0%   25%   50%   75%   100%
```

### Figure 3 — Evidence-to-hypothesis transport

A matrix or graph showing which evidence framings increase which downstream problem/method framings, evaluated on held-out topics.

### Figure 4 — Sampling breadth vs search direction

Show that temperature / generation-diversity intervention changes within-context spread but does not eliminate between-context directional shifts.

### Figure 5 — Optional discovery utility

MUSES intellectual root vs relevance-matched non-root, only if temporally clean.

### One-sentence main claim

> **Retrieved evidence is not a neutral knowledge supplement: its composition acts as a structured inference-time prior that steers where LLMs search for scientific hypotheses, an effect that can be invisible to standard duplicate-based diversity metrics.**

This is currently the strongest ICLR framing.

## 12. Relationship to the macro “AI narrows science” question

A 2026 Nature study over 41.3M natural-science papers reports that AI-augmented scientists have higher individual output/impact while collective topic breadth contracts. This is strong motivation, but it must **not** be presented as evidence for our mechanism.

Our possible contribution is much narrower and more defensible:

> shared evidence infrastructure is a candidate micro-level mechanism that can be manipulated experimentally in LLM systems.

Connecting it causally to real-world science would require a separate human/ecosystem study.

## 13. Current research decision

### GO — but only for the revised question

Proceed to pilot infrastructure **only after Amendment B is read**.

The scientific target is no longer:

> top-k retrieval reduces diversity.

It is:

> **at fixed amount and matched relevance, evidence composition changes the structured hypothesis distribution; coarse duplicate metrics may fail to reveal this effect.**

This hypothesis is more falsifiable, less obvious, more directly tied to LLM inference behavior, and better separated from current ICLR/2026 scientific-ideation work.
